# ITRDB empirical Stage B figure captions v01

These are provisional captions. Numerical values must be inserted only from the
frozen empirical outputs generated by `run_itrdb_empirical_stage_b_v01.py`.

## Figure E1. Conventional plug-in EPS and covariance dimension

Conventional plug-in expressed population signal (EPS) against effective
correlation-matrix dimension divided by the number of trees for the three public
ITRDB datasets after primary AgeDepSpline detrending and equal-weight
core-to-tree aggregation. Effective dimension is a structural descriptor, not a
reliability coefficient. The fixed complete periods and tree counts are given in
Table Sx.

## Figure E2. Subset-to-full sensitivity profiles

Median subset-to-full correlation (line), 5th–95th percentile interval (shading),
and sampled minimum (points) as a function of subset tree count. Each subset is
compared with the full chronology that contains it, so the diagnostic includes
shared biological units and is not an independent replication test. At most 250
unique compositions were retained per subset size.

## Figure E3. Disjoint split-half replication

Median (point), 5th–95th percentile interval (line), and sampled minimum (cross)
of correlations between 1,000 unique exhaustive, non-overlapping, balanced
tree-group partitions. Group sizes differ by one because each empirical cohort
contains an odd number of trees. Unlike subset-to-full comparisons, the two
chronologies share no biological units.

## Figure E4. Tree-bootstrap variance sensitivity

Median and 5th–95th percentile interval of the bootstrap/full chronology
variance ratio from 2,000 explicit N-out-of-N tree-bootstrap draws. Bootstrap and
full chronologies share the represented trees; direct differences and variance
ratios are therefore emphasized over their correlation.

## Figure E5. Distinct domains of single-tree influence

Maximum standardized RMS chronology change versus maximum absolute change in
conventional plug-in EPS under leave-one-tree-out analysis. The two maxima need
not identify the same tree, because influence on the chronology trajectory and
influence on mean covariance/replication are different estimands.
